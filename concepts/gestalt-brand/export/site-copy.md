# Gestalt landing page — full site copy

Every piece of visible text on the scroll driven landing page, in page order.
Edit here, then apply changes back to `website/index.html`.

This version is written for **DesignPhiladelphia Awards voters in the
Accessibility category**, and for readers scanning for cultural innovation.
The through-line: the tools that read the practical world stopped at the curb
of culture, and Gestalt is the software built past it. The argument is never
stated as a pitch; the demo makes it. Warm, human, plain. No dashes, no talk
of AI, no competitor names, no "innovative" or "first."

**Standing correction:** descriptions are human-reviewed, NOT artist-approved.
Safe claims: written by a describer, reviewed by a human, artist credited by
name. Never "artist approved," "the artists' words," or "permission on record."

## 03 — What art does to a person (`#showcase`)

**Headline:** A wall you understand is a wall you belong to.

**Body ¶1:** A mural isn't decoration. It's an argument, a memory, a joke, a
grief, made public on purpose. Taking one in should reach everyone on the
sidewalk, not just the people who can read it at a glance.

**Body ¶2:** For years, software has been able to say what sits in front of
you. The tools that read the practical world can name a menu, a dollar bill,
even a painted wall, and there they stop. Gestalt was built for what comes
after: a description written the way people actually write about art, tied to
the exact wall you're standing at, reviewed by a human describer before it
ever plays.

**Stats:**
| Term | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Written as | Art writing, not captions |
| Reviewed by | A human describer |
| Cost to a visitor | $0 |

**Headline:** What a visitor hears.

**Intro (above the player):** *Persistence*, a mural by Jason Andrew Turner: a
towering blue wall, a figure in a red dress scattered with white dots. A

## 05 — Who it's for (`#workflow`, pinned one card at a time gallery)

*(The former "What it changes" six-card section was cut entirely; its
strongest claims already live in the showcase body, the workflow cards, and
the demo captions. Em dashes below are list separators in this doc only.)*

**Headline:** Four neighbors. One wall.

1. **Blind and low vision Philadelphians** — The city's public art on your own
   terms, described the moment you stand in front of it. No help required, no
   one to ask.
2. **Neighbors who don't read English** — A mural explained in a language you
   actually read, so the city's walls speak to all of it, not part.
3. **The city and its institutions** — A live, honest picture of what's
   described and what isn't. The ADA accountability a public collection owes,
   ready the morning of the meeting.
4. **Artists and Mural Arts partners** — Every description carries the
   artist's name and points people back to the work. The wall stays the
   artist's; Gestalt makes sure more of the city arrives at it.

## Editor's note (not on the page)

- **Register kept from the category's best:** independence, never charity.
  Users as agents. Matter-of-fact capability. No inspiration framing.
- **Deliberately different from the category:** no sight metaphors anywhere
  (the biggest name in the space leads with one; our restraint is legible
  craft to this jury). Subject is culture, not errands.
- **The unnamed nod:** "the tools that read the practical world" is the one
  soft reference to the assistive-tech landscape. It is never expanded.
- **Claims audit:** no artist approval, no permission on record, no "first,"
  no "innovative," no WCAG numbers, no competitor names. The one legal claim
  (ADA monitoring) survives because it's a product fact, not a boast.
