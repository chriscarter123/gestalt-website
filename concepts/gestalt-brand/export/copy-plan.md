# Copy revision plan — "the invention" framing

Audience: DesignPhiladelphia Awards voters, Accessibility category, plus
readers scanning for cultural innovation. Goal: the page should read as the
story of software invented for a problem nobody had built for, without ever
saying the words innovative, first, or award.

---

## 1 · What the comparators taught us

Be My Eyes ("Let's see the world together"), Envision ("OCR that speaks out
the visual world"), and Supersense ("Computer vision for the blind") share one
register worth keeping and one gap worth owning.

**Keep their register.** All three write independence, not charity.
Matter-of-fact capability. Users as agents, never recipients. No inspiration
framing. Our copy already leans this way; the revision holds that line
everywhere.

**Own their gap.** All three are general-purpose utilities for the practical
world: mail, menus, currency, barcodes, scenes. Their scene description can
tell you there is a painted wall. None of them can tell you it's an argument,
who made it, or why it's on that corner. Assistive tech solved errands and
stopped at the curb of culture. That is the invention story: Gestalt is
software built past that curb, for the thing the general tools treat as
background.

**The differentiators, concretely:**
- Subject: art, not tasks. Written description as art writing, not OCR output.
- Depth: written and human-reviewed, not a generic caption generated on the
  spot. (Correction on record: descriptions are NOT artist-approved — never
  claim approval or permission. Artist credit by name is the safe claim.)
- Place: outdoors, in the weather, tied to the specific wall you're standing
  at. The one venue with no front desk, no docent, no device cart.
- Direction: the practical apps bring the world to the user's phone; Gestalt
  was built so the city itself carries its own description.

## 2 · How on-the-nose is too on-the-nose

Never on the page: competitor names, "innovative," "first ever," "vote,"
"nominee deserves," WCAG numbers, or any lecture about accessibility law
beyond the one ADA line that's already earned its place. Jurors in this
category can smell a pitch; what wins them is craft plus a real demo.

Allowed, once each, softly: "the tools that read the practical world" as an
unnamed nod to the category landscape; "nobody had built" phrasing in the
showcase body; the DesignPhiladelphia meta line that already exists in the
hero (factual, not a plea).

The demo stays the argument. Everything before it earns the press of the play
button; everything after it names what just happened.

## 3 · Section-by-section

- **Hero (01).** Keep "Art belongs to everyone. Now it's finally accessible."
  It already reads as a category thesis without naming the category. Rework
  the lede one notch toward invention: a phone already reads your mail; now
  point it at a wall. Keep deck, buttons, meta line as they are.

- **Premise (02).** Untouched. "Access is not generosity..." is the strongest
  accessibility-jury line on the page precisely because it isn't pitching.

- **Showcase (03).** This section carries the new argument. ¶1 stays the
  cultural claim (a mural is an argument, a memory, a joke, a grief). ¶2
  becomes the gap: software has long been able to say what is in front of
  you; it was never taught to say what it means. Then what was actually
  built: descriptions written as art writing, tied to the wall, approved by
  the people who made the work. Consider retitling the stats row terms to
  match (e.g. "Written by" / "Approved by" / "Cost to a visitor").

- **Hear it (04).** Light touch only. Add one sentence of contrast to the
  intro: a generic caption would call Persistence a blue wall; then the
  visitor presses play and hears what a describer wrote. Card and closing
  stay.

- **What it changes (05).** Reorder so invention leads: (1) written for a
  person, not read off a label; (2) made for the street where no guide ever
  went; (3) nothing to borrow, nothing to disclose; (4) languages; (5) fuller
  encounter for everyone; (6) ADA record. Tighten each to one breath.

- **Who it's for (06).** Keep all four cards; sharpen card 1 so independence
  is unmistakable (on your own terms, no help required — already close).

- **Take the tour (07).** Keep as the closing CTA. Add one clause connecting
  it to the thesis (the whole city, described) rather than app-store speak.

- **Footer.** Keep "Every artwork has a voice." Fix the two placeholder
  accessibility links (conformance, known limits) or cut them; a broken
  promise here costs more with this jury than no promise.

## 4 · Voice non-negotiables (unchanged)

Identity-first language. No sight metaphors (note: Be My Eyes leads with one;
we deliberately don't — that restraint is legible to this jury). "Audio
description," never narration. No AI talk. No dashes. No exclamation points.
One italic gold flourish. Warm, plain, contractions fine.
